# Sway Product Brief

## Positioning

**Sway helps you discover what you actually like, then helps you choose.**

The product combines the immediacy of visual pairwise voting with an AI decision partner. It is playful enough to browse casually and structured enough to reach a real outcome.

## Audience

Gen Z and young millennial consumers who:

- discover products visually;
- find long search-result lists exhausting;
- struggle to describe their taste in words;
- want recommendations without surrendering the final decision; and
- share options with friends before buying or booking.

## Core loop

```text
Agent proposes candidates
        ↓
User makes a visual choice
        ↓
Community result is revealed
        ↓
Agent updates a transparent taste profile
        ↓
Next comparison becomes more relevant
        ↓
User approves a shortlist or final choice
```

## Experience principles

### One tap first

The first interaction should require no form, explanation, or account. Users can supply context when it becomes useful.

### Curiosity creates momentum

Community percentages appear only after the user chooses, preserving the “what did everyone else pick?” reward.

### AI earns trust visibly

The agent must cite the user's actual choices when proposing a taste insight. Inferences remain editable and reversible.

### Fun leads to utility

Explore mode builds a taste profile through casual choices. Decide mode applies that profile to a real goal such as choosing a hotel or outfit.

### The user decides

The agent narrows, explains, and recommends. The human accepts the shortlist and makes consequential purchase or booking decisions.

## Challenge MVP: travel

Demo prompt:

> Help me choose a design-forward Lisbon hotel for a weekend under $350 per night.

End-to-end flow:

1. The user states the trip goal.
2. The agent adds six structured hotel candidates.
3. Sway presents five deliberate pairwise comparisons.
4. After each choice, the user sees community sentiment.
5. The agent proposes two or three taste insights with supporting choices.
6. Price and location are revealed for the strongest candidates.
7. The user can revise a preference after seeing practical tradeoffs.
8. The agent creates a three-option shortlist.
9. The user approves the final recommendation.

## Category system

| Category | Candidate type | Taste signals | Practical constraints |
|---|---|---|---|
| Travel | Hotels and stays | Mood, architecture, scale, setting | Price, dates, location, amenities |
| Fashion | Garments and outfits | Silhouette, color, styling, brand | Size, budget, occasion, availability |
| Beauty | Products and looks | Finish, palette, packaging, intensity | Skin type, ingredients, routine, price |
| Home | Furniture and rooms | Era, material, palette, density | Dimensions, budget, durability |
| Food | Restaurants and dishes | Cuisine, presentation, atmosphere | Diet, distance, price, availability |
| Gifts | Products and experiences | Sentiment, novelty, aesthetic | Recipient, occasion, timing, budget |

## Engagement system

- A small curated daily set, not an endless obligation.
- Instant result reveal after each choice.
- Personal taste insights that become more precise over time.
- Friend compatibility: “You and Maya matched on 78% of this board.”
- Shareable boards and shortlists rather than public popularity metrics about people.
- Community-created comparison boards with moderation and attribution.
- No manipulative notification volume or hidden sponsored ranking.

## Safety and trust

- Do not infer sensitive personal traits from aesthetic choices.
- Keep taste profiles private by default.
- Label sponsored and affiliate content clearly.
- Require human confirmation before deletion, purchase, booking, posting, or sharing.
- Use licensed or authorized product data and imagery.
- Let users inspect, correct, reset, and export their preferences.

## Measures of success

- Comparison completion rate
- Time to useful shortlist
- Percentage of taste insights accepted or corrected
- Return rate for a second category or decision
- Shortlist save/share rate
- User-reported confidence in the final choice
